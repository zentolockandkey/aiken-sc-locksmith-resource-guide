Residential Lock Services: Homes Across Aiken County

Aiken has a wide range of housing stock — from historic cottages near Downtown Aiken to newer construction in Gem Lakes and Southwood subdivisions. Lock needs vary just as much as the properties themselves.

Rekeying vs. lock replacement

Rekeying changes the internal pin configuration of an existing lock so that old keys no longer work. It costs less than a full lock replacement and is the right call when the hardware is still in good shape — common after buying a home, ending a lease, or losing a key. Lock replacement makes more sense when the deadbolt is worn, the latch is loose, or you want to upgrade to a higher-security cylinder.

Smart lock installation is growing in popularity across Aiken. A keypad or app-controlled deadbolt lets you grant one-time entry codes for contractors, dog walkers, or guests — no key duplication required. Battery backup means a power outage will not leave you stranded.

After-move-in security checklist for Aiken homeowners:

  • Rekey or replace all exterior locks, including the garage entry door.
  • Test every deadbolt — make sure the bolt extends fully and seats cleanly in the strike plate.
  • Reinforce strike plates with 3-inch screws that reach the door frame stud.
  • Check sliding doors for secondary security pins or a bar in the track.
  • Confirm window locks work on all ground-floor and accessible windows.
  • Decide who holds spare keys and store one outside in a quality key lockbox, not under a mat.

How to Spot a Locksmith Scam

Locksmith fraud is a real problem across South Carolina and the rest of the country. Knowing the warning signs protects you when you are already stressed from a lockout.

  • Extremely low advertised prices: A quote of $15 or $25 to unlock a door is almost always a bait-and-switch. Expect a real price once the technician arrives and refuses to honor the original quote.
  • No local address: Legitimate locksmiths have a verifiable business address. National call centers that dispatch unknown subcontractors often have no local accountability.
  • Unmarked vehicles: A professional locksmith typically arrives in a labeled vehicle with proper tools — not a personal car with a generic toolbox.
  • Pressure to drill immediately: Most lock openings do not require drilling. A technician who reaches for a drill without trying to pick or bypass the lock first should raise a red flag.
  • No written estimate: Ask for a total price before any work begins. A trustworthy locksmith should be willing to explain pricing before work begins.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is rekeying a lock as secure as replacing it?

Yes, in most cases. Rekeying changes the key combination by swapping the internal pins, so previous keys become useless. The security of a rekeyed lock depends on the quality of the lock itself. If your existing deadbolt is a solid Grade 1 or Grade 2 lock, rekeying it is just as effective as buying a new one. If the lock is old, worn, or a low-grade knob set, replacing it is the smarter move.

Can a locksmith make a new car key if I have lost all my copies?

Yes. A locksmith with automotive key programming equipment can generate a new key from the vehicle identification number (VIN) and program it to your car's immobilizer — even with zero working keys on hand. This is called a "key from scratch" job and typically requires proof of ownership. It takes more time than a duplicate cut, but it avoids an expensive tow to a dealership.

Do I need a locksmith for a smart lock installation, or can I do it myself?

Many smart locks are designed for DIY installation on a standard door prep. However, a locksmith is worth calling if your door has a non-standard bore, if the existing deadbolt is misaligned, or if you want the lock properly integrated with a home automation system. A professional can also make sure the strike plate is reinforced correctly — a step that DIY guides often skip but that matters more than the lock itself.
